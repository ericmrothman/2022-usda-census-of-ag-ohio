# Ohio Agriculture Explorer

An interactive explorer built from `ohv1.txt` — the USDA NASS **2022 Census of
Agriculture, Ohio, Volume 1 (Geographic Area Series, Part 35, AC-22-A-35)**.

**Open [`ohio_ag_explorer.html`](ohio_ag_explorer.html) in a browser.** That's the
whole tool: one 0.9 MB file with the data, the charts and the map inside it. No
server, no network, no install. It works from a USB stick.

---

## What's in it

**2,325 measures × 88 counties**, plus Ohio totals and a 1997–2022 state series.
Everything the report publishes at county level: farm counts and acreage, sales
by commodity, production expenses, government payments, livestock inventories,
every crop from corn to Marionberries, machinery, organic acreage, and producer
demographics.

### 18 views, in three shelves

The tab bar is split into three labelled rows, because eighteen tabs in one
strip all read as equally likely: **Start here** (where to go before you know
what you want), **Single measure** (the sidebar's measure, drawn a different
way), and **Compare measures** (two or more things side by side). Views needing
a second measure dim and show a `+1` until one is picked.

Every control triggers a full re-render, so the things you have adjusted about
the page itself are carried across it: which sidebar sections are open, both
panels' scroll positions, any scrollable list inside the view, and the text and
caret in a filter box. Switching to a different view still starts you at its top.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Key findings** | One-click presets that open the explorer on a specific finding |
| **All measures** | The measure dictionary — browse all 2,325 by topic and table |
| **Map** | Choropleth on an equal-area projection |
| **Tile grid** | Every county one equal tile — so Cuyahoga doesn't vanish next to Darke |
| **Two at once** | Bivariate choropleth: two measures, one map, 3×3 key |
| **Ranked bars** | Sorted magnitude, diverging when values carry a sign |

### Controls

- **Measure picker** — search 2,325 measures by crop, animal, dollar figure or table,
  or open the **All measures** dictionary and browse them instead (see below)
- **Year** — 2017 / 2022, or the change between them (absolute or %)
- **How to count it** — raw · % of Ohio · per farm · per farm acre · per 1,000 acres
  of land · z-score · rank · **location quotient** (specialisation)
- **Counties** — keep-only or leave-out, search, invert, Appalachian Ohio (ARC),
  top/bottom N, and your own saved collections (kept in `localStorage`).
  Filtering lives here and only here — clicking a county on a chart **spotlights**
  it (ring on the map, others dimmed elsewhere, and it re-anchors the twins list),
  it never removes it from the data. Click again to clear.
- **Colour** — 7 sequential ramps, 3 diverging pairs, 3–9 classes, and four ways to
  place the class edges (equal count / equal range / natural breaks / log)
- **Export** — SVG, 2× PNG, or the CSV behind the current view
- `[` and `]` step through views; the URL hash captures the full state, so any
  view you land on is a link you can paste to someone.

### The measure dictionary

Search only works once you know what to ask for. The **All measures** tab is the
other way in: the whole report laid out as the census organises it — topic → one
of the 57 county tables → section → measure — with everything collapsed until you
open it. Each entry shows the years it covers, how many of the 88 counties report
it, and how many cells were withheld; clicking selects it and jumps to the map,
and the **vs** chip on hover puts it in the comparison slot. There is a **Surprise
me** button for when you want the census to pick.

Filtering the dictionary auto-expands whatever matches, so `goat` opens the five
goat tables at once. When nothing matches it says so, and points out that the
census uses its own vocabulary — "hogs" not "pigs", "equine" not "horses",
"floriculture" not "flowers".

### Key findings (presets)

The first tab. Pick any county and it produces preset cards, each stating a
finding and carrying a **Show me →** button that configures the whole explorer to
prove it. Presets reset every setting they don't set, so they land identically
every time.

Three sections are **generated by scanning all 2,325 measures**, so they work for
any of the 88 counties: where the county *leads Ohio*, where it *punches above its
weight* (location quotient against its share of farms), and what *moved most since
2017*. Measures reported by fewer than 45 counties, statewide totals under 500, and
county figures under 12 farms are excluded — at those counts a single operation
decides the ranking — and restatements of the same figure are folded together.

**Montgomery County (Dayton)** also has six hand-picked cards. Their prose is
fixed; every number in them is read live from the data, so the text cannot drift
from the figures:

- **The headline is misleading** — 51st of 88 on total sales ($117.5M), but 6th in
  Ohio on nursery/greenhouse/floriculture ($22.9M). That's 19% of the county's own
  farm sales against 4% for Ohio.
- **Under glass** — 187,032 sq ft of greenhouse vegetables and fresh-cut herbs,
  3rd highest in the state, 7.6% of Ohio's total.
- **Who farms here** — the most land farmed by Hispanic, Latino or Spanish-origin
  producers of any Ohio county (11,487 acres, 10% of the state), plus 2nd highest
  for Black or African American producers.
- **What it grows** — first in Ohio for beets, spinach and turnips; top three for
  carrots and lettuce. Any one crop is thin evidence; the repetition is the signal.
- **What changed** — contract labour spending went from $202,000 to $2.3 million
  (+1,044%), which is what a shift toward hand-picked crops costs.
- **Its peers** — its statistical twins are Delaware, Morrow, Lorain, Ottawa and
  Warren: the metro-edge ring, not the farm-belt west.

The through-line is that Montgomery is a **metro market-garden county**. Ranked by
total sales it looks unremarkable; ranked by what it actually grows, it doesn't.

### Two things the report itself can't tell you

- **"What else looks like this?"** — correlates your measure against all 2,325
  others across the 88 counties and ranks what moves *with* it and *against* it.
  Click any row to plot the pair. (Sanity check: it finds r = 1.000 for figures the
  census printed in two different tables.)
- **"Counties most like X"** — nearest neighbours across ten standardised headline
  measures. Anchor it on Holmes and you get Knox, Licking, Muskingum, Coshocton and
  Tuscarawas — its actual Amish-country neighbours — with Darke, the industrial
  grain county, furthest away.

## Publishing to GitHub Pages

The explorer is one static file with no build step, no server and no external
requests, so Pages serves it as-is. `bundle.py` writes the same bundle to both
`ohio_ag_explorer.html` and **`index.html`** — the latter is what Pages serves.
(Identical content means git stores one blob, so the duplicate is free.)

**One time:**

```bash
git init && git add -A && git commit -m "Ohio Agriculture Explorer"
git branch -M main
git remote add origin https://github.com/<you>/<repo>.git
git push -u origin main
```

Then on GitHub: **Settings → Pages → Source: Deploy from a branch →
Branch: `main` / `(root)` → Save.** The site appears at
`https://<you>.github.io/<repo>/` within a minute or two.

**Every time you change something:**

```bash
python3 bundle.py && git commit -am "update" && git push
```

Notes:

- `.nojekyll` is committed so Pages skips Jekyll and copies the file straight
  through. Nothing here is Jekyll-shaped, and it makes deploys quicker.
- **No Actions workflow is needed.** The bundle is committed, so there is nothing
  to build on the server.
- `data/` (56 MB of parsed CSVs) and the packed payload are `.gitignore`d —
  they regenerate from `ohv1.txt`, which *is* committed, so the repo stays ~15 MB
  and the pipeline is still reproducible from a fresh clone.
- The URL hash carries the full view state, so any chart you land on is a
  shareable deep link — e.g. `…/#view=treemap&mode=lq&ramp=teal`.
- Pages is HTTPS-only, which `DecompressionStream` requires anyway. Verified: all
  17 views render served over HTTP with **zero external network requests**.

### `parse_ohv1.py`

`ohv1.txt` is a 72,000-line fixed-width print report, not a data file. The parser
works from the document's own structure rather than hard-coded column widths:

- A table is printed as a run of **panels** delimited by rule lines. The last line
  of a panel's header carries the column labels, and the byte offsets of its `:`
  characters define the field boundaries for every row beneath it.
- Two layouts occur, told apart by the header's stub cell: `Item` (measures down
  the side, places across the top) and `Geographic area` (the transpose). Both are
  normalised to one `(geo, metric, unit, year) -> value` shape.
- Multi-line headers are merged, distinguishing a **spanner** (covers several leaf
  fields → `Inventory > Farms`) from a **wrapped label** (covers one → joined with
  a space).
- Narrow tables are printed **two-up**, the halves divided by `::`. Each half is
  parsed in its own character window; without this, values from the right-hand
  table land on the wrong county.
- Item labels wrap across lines. A line is a section heading if it ends in `:` or
  `-`, is a `- Con.` reprint, or is set in full caps; anything else is the first
  line of a label that wrapped.
- Section paths come from indentation, and rows that carry values are parents too —
  so `Corn` resolves to the same path in the first panel and in a continuation
  reprint after a page break.
- Census codes are preserved as flags: `(D)` withheld to avoid disclosing an
  individual operation, `(Z)` rounds to zero, `(NA)`, `(X)`, and `-` (a true zero).

**Validation.** 610+ additive measures were checked against the Ohio totals the
report publishes independently: **every one reconciles exactly**, with zero
unparsed cells across 247,086 county observations. `parse_ohv1.py` prints the
counts; the reconciliation check lives in the commit history and is quick to rerun.

Outputs in `data/`:

| file | rows | what |
|---|---|---|
| `county_long.csv` | 247,086 | tidy `geo × metric × unit × year × value × flag` |
| `state_history.csv` | 904 | Ohio, 1992–2022, from the state chapter's Table 1 |
| `state_breakdown.csv` | 81,517 | Ohio totals by size / class / tenure etc. |
| `counties.json`, `metrics.json` | | the county list and the measure catalogue |

Land-use figures live in county Table 8 (*Farms, Land in Farms, Value of Land and
Buildings, and Land Use*) and Table 41 (*Land Use Practices*) — search the measure
picker for "land", or filter `county_long.csv` on `table_key` `c2t8` / `c2t41`.

## Reading the numbers honestly

- **Blanks are not zeros.** The census withholds any figure that would identify an
  individual operation. 22,286 county cells are withheld; charts leave them out and
  say so, and the county sums under a suppressed measure fall short of the Ohio
  total by exactly the withheld amount.
- **`$1,000` is a scale, not a currency symbol.** A sales figure of `15,412,137`
  means $15.4 billion.
- **Averages and medians don't add up.** "Per farm" and "average size" columns are
  ratios; summing them across counties is meaningless, and the reconciliation check
  excludes them for that reason.
- **Correlation across 88 counties is not cause.** Most measures correlate strongly
  simply because they all track how much farmland a county has — which is why the
  "How to count it" control matters. Switch to *per farm* or *location quotient*
  and most of the spurious agreement disappears.
- **1992 is missing from the time series** — the report publishes it unadjusted for
  coverage only, so it isn't comparable with the later censuses and is excluded.

## Design notes

Colour follows a validated palette: sequential ramps are single-hue light→dark,
diverging ramps are a warm/cool pair around a neutral grey, and the 8-slot
categorical order clears CVD and normal-vision separation checks in both light and
dark themes. Dark mode is a separate set of steps chosen for the dark surface, not
an inverted light mode. Labels printed inside marks pick black or white by measured
contrast ratio — every in-mark label clears 4.5:1 in both themes. Every view has a
table twin, and no value is reachable only through a tooltip.
